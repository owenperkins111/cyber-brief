Morning. It's Sunday the eighteenth of October, and this is your Cyber Brief.

Right, a quieter weekend on paper, but one story landed on Saturday that anyone running Microsoft 365 for other people needs to deal with today. Let's get into it.

First, a supply chain attack through a backup tool. ConnectWise Cloud Backup, the product that used to be called SkyKick, is used by MSPs to back up client mailboxes and files in Microsoft 365. Somebody got hold of the app's own credential and used it to sign straight into customer tenants as the backup application. Because that's an app sign-in, not a user one, MFA never came into it. Huntress says it's confirmed unauthorised sign-ins in more than a hundred and sixty tenants, and Microsoft calls it an active supply chain attack. In most tenants researchers saw sign-ins and reconnaissance, but in one, a related ConnectWise management app gave itself Global Administrator and added fresh secrets to other trusted apps, which is a way back in after the original credential's reset. ConnectWise has switched off the app's access and says it's seen no sign that backup data itself was touched. How the credential leaked hasn't been disclosed. So what do you do? Disable the ConnectWise and SkyKick apps in every tenant you manage, check service principal sign-ins rather than user sign-ins, and look for any new app secrets added since the thirteenth. And remember, revoking user sessions does nothing here, and tokens already issued can live on for up to ninety minutes.

And while we're on remote access tools, BeyondTrust has patched a zero-day in Remote Support, rated nine point three. Google's Mandiant team says a China-linked espionage group exploited it from late August, and it's the same crew previously tied to Citrix and Ivanti zero-days. Once in, they dropped a web shell, got root, pulled credentials out of the appliance's secrets store, and used them to hop onto the downstream systems the appliance manages. Then they deleted logs. SaaS customers were patched on the ninth. Self-hosted customers have to apply the patch themselves, and older releases need an upgrade first. CISA's added it to its exploited list with a federal deadline of tomorrow. If you had an internet-facing box on a vulnerable version, treat it as compromised until you've looked.

Elsewhere, Drupal has fixed two critical remote code execution bugs, one in the core Field UI module and one in the contributed Entity List module. Neither is exploitable through core alone, but some paths need no login at all, and Drupal's own team warned exploits could appear within hours or days. There's no confirmed exploitation yet. Update to the latest point release on your branch, and if you can't, uninstall Field UI as a stopgap. Anything older than the currently supported branches won't get a fix.

On breaches here in the UK, the investigation at the owner of Hobbs, Whistles and Phase Eight is still going nearly a week on. The company's confirmed IT systems are down, stores are trading, and it says it's got no reason to think payment data was at risk. Nobody's claimed it and data theft isn't confirmed. It's the latest in a long run of UK fashion and retail incidents, so if you look after retail, the help desk and identity checks remain the place to spend your effort.

One more before we move on. The Register has a good piece on attackers going after the physical side of buildings: heating and cooling, door access, refrigeration. The headline example is this month's attack in France, where thousands of remote-managed fridge controllers across hundreds of supermarkets were pushed to dangerous temperatures overnight. Another firm described an extortion crew locking a client out of its own offices after it refused to pay. Researchers say building systems make a great hiding place too, because hardly anyone monitors them. Worth asking where your building management kit actually sits on the network.

Right, AI and security. A coding tool called OpenCode has confirmed a breach of a legacy system it was retiring, affecting about one and a half percent of its Console users. The worry is the third party AI provider keys those users had stored, for OpenAI, Anthropic and Google. OpenCode says the keys may have been exposed, and it's linked the incident to a large scale distillation attack, which is where someone hammers a model to harvest its answers and train their own. Some users are reporting surprise bills on social media, though that's unverified. The so-what is simple. AI API keys are money and data, so store them in a proper secrets manager, set spending caps, and watch usage like you'd watch cloud billing. If you got the email, rotate now.

Now, the business side. The UK's Cyber Security and Resilience Bill is due back in the Lords for report stage, reportedly pencilled in for the twenty-sixth. It brings managed service providers and data centres into scope, with faster incident reporting, and Royal Assent's expected somewhere between late this year and next spring. If you're building a services business, expect clients to start asking whether you're in scope, and get your own incident reporting story straight before they do. Separately, Crunchbase says security startups raised six point eight billion dollars in the third quarter, up about half on the quarter before, but from fewer, bigger rounds, mostly for AI and identity. The take? Money's chasing scale, so smaller firms win on specialism and service, not on breadth.

And today's one to hunt. Off the back of the ConnectWise story, go and look at what your third party apps are doing in your identity platform. Specifically, look for any service principal, any app identity, that adds itself or another app to an admin role, or adds a new secret or certificate to an application. Those are rare, high value events, and in normal operation they're done by a human admin, not by an app. If an app is handing itself privileges or keys, that's your alert. While you're there, prune old app consents you no longer use.

So, three things for today. One, disable the ConnectWise and SkyKick apps in every Microsoft 365 tenant and audit for new app secrets since the thirteenth. Two, patch self-hosted BeyondTrust Remote Support and check it for signs of compromise. Three, update Drupal sites now, or pull the Field UI module until you can.

That's your lot. Have a good one.
