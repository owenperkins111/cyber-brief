Morning. It's Tuesday the 13th of October, and this is your Cyber Brief.

Right, let's start with the calendar, because today's a big one. It's Patch Tuesday, and Microsoft's October release lands early this evening UK time. But today's also an end of support date, and that matters more for a lot of estates. Office 2021 stops getting security fixes today. So do Windows 11 Home and Pro on version 24H2, which means those machines need to move to a newer feature release. And the third and final year of paid extended security updates for Windows Server 2012 and 2012 R2 runs out, with no fourth year announced. So if you've still got 2012 boxes tucked away somewhere, tonight's patches are the last they'll ever get. Worth pulling an inventory today, not next month.

And while we're on edge devices, SonicWall. Last Tuesday it patched a maximum severity flaw, a ten out of ten, in its SMA 1000 remote access appliances. It's a server-side request forgery bug in the WorkPlace interface that lets an unauthenticated attacker make the appliance send requests on their behalf and reach internal functions. SonicWall didn't flag it as exploited at the time, but two threat intel firms, Previdian and Defused, say their honeypots started seeing attempts within two days. It's not yet confirmed that any of those attempts actually compromised a real device. It's also the third bug of this kind in the same interface this year. If you run SMA 1000, patch now and check for anything odd since the sixth.

Quick one on Citrix as well. Late last week it warned about another critical NetScaler ADC and Gateway bug, a memory overflow scored nine point five that could lead to remote code execution. It only bites on appliances set up as a SAML service provider or identity provider. Citrix says it isn't aware of exploitation yet, but given the run of NetScaler zero-days over the past month, I wouldn't wait for that to change.

Now, breaches and incidents. Ahold Delhaize, the Dutch-Belgian grocery giant behind Albert Heijn, Delhaize, Food Lion and Stop and Shop, has taken some systems offline after what it calls a security issue in its network. That much is confirmed. Stores are open, but Albert Heijn's website, app and home delivery went down, and staff at some US chains reported trouble reaching schedules and pay records. The company hasn't said whether it's ransomware or whether data was taken, and nobody's claimed it yet. Another big retailer pulling the plug as a precaution. Sound familiar?

Closer to home for UK shoppers, H&M says it's investigating claims of a cyber incident involving what it calls a limited scope of its data. Reports link those claims to ShinyHunters. H&M says it's seen no sign so far that its data environment was compromised, so for now this is a claim, not a confirmed breach. It comes a week after ASOS confirmed that someone accessed customer names and contact details through a third-party customer messaging platform. Fashion retail is clearly a target right now.

Elsewhere, a phishing campaign that defenders, and especially managed service providers, should know about. Huntress has been tracking emails that link to a real Microsoft Power BI page. Because it's Microsoft's own domain, mail filters tend to wave it through. The page offers a download, the visitor gets fingerprinted, and if they look like a real Windows user they get a rogue ScreenConnect installer. That first remote access client then quietly swaps itself for a second one tied to different attacker infrastructure, with a scheduled task to keep it alive. No malware in the usual sense, just a legitimate remote support tool doing the attacker's work.

Right, AI and security. Researchers at Zenity have published how a single prompt could turn an AWS Bedrock AgentCore agent against its own cloud account. A public-facing agent with a web request tool could be told to fetch the cloud metadata endpoint, which handed back the temporary credentials for the agent's role. Because the default role was broad, those credentials reached other agents, their chat history, and stored secrets. AWS says reading your own role's credentials is expected behaviour, but it has since tightened the default permissions and moved to the safer version of the metadata service. The so-what? Prompt injection plus a network tool equals server-side request forgery. Give every agent its own narrow role, block metadata access, and keep public agents away from internal ones.

Now, the business side. IonQ, the quantum computing company, is buying zero trust firm Elisity in an all-stock deal worth up to one point three billion dollars. Elisity controls which users, devices and AI agents can talk to each other on a network, and IonQ wants its customer relationships to sell post-quantum cryptography. The take for anyone building a services business: crypto inventory and post-quantum migration planning is turning into a real line item, and someone has to do the discovery work.

And a stranger one. The FBI has arrested a Canadian ransomware response specialist in its investigation into ShinyHunters, including the group's claimed breach of FBI systems. He's charged with extortion-related offences, which are allegations, not proven, and reports suggest others in the negotiation business could face scrutiny. The take: clients hand incident responders enormous trust. Vetting, two-person rules and clean audit trails on negotiation work aren't optional extras anymore.

And today's one to hunt. Tied to that Power BI campaign: look for remote access tools you didn't put there. Build an approved list of the remote support and RMM products you actually use, including the specific cloud instances or tenant IDs, and alert on any new install or service that doesn't match. Then go a step further and flag any machine running two different remote access clients, or a scheduled task that keeps relaunching one every few minutes. Attackers love legitimate tools because they blend in, so the tell is usually that it's the wrong instance, not the wrong product.

So, three things for today. One, check what's losing support today, especially Server 2012 and Windows 11 24H2 Home and Pro, and line up tonight's Patch Tuesday. Two, if you run SonicWall SMA 1000, patch it and look back over the past week. Three, audit your remote access tools against an approved list, because rogue ScreenConnect installs are arriving via trusted Microsoft links.

That's your lot. Have a good one.
