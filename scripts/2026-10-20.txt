Morning. It's Tuesday the 20th of October, and this is your Cyber Brief.

Right, let's start with the one every MSP needs to look at today. ConnectWise has suspended its Cloud Backup service, the product many of you will know as SkyKick, after attackers got hold of the credential behind its Microsoft 365 app. That app is hugely privileged. It can read mailboxes, OneDrive, SharePoint, Teams and directory data in every tenant that's consented to it. Huntress says it saw unauthorised sign-ins across a hundred and seventy-three partner and customer tenants, with tokens pulled via Microsoft Graph on the morning of the 15th, moving through tenants a few seconds apart, which looks scripted. Microsoft has called it an active supply chain attack, and says it saw Graph reconnaissance and attempts to add credentials as a backdoor. In at least one tenant, a related ConnectWise app added itself to Global Administrator. ConnectWise says it's found no evidence partner backup data was compromised, and it's also paused its SaaS Security and Cloud Management services, which connect to Cloud Backup. So what do you do? Disable the app in every tenant until ConnectWise confirms all credentials have been reset, check service principal sign-ins for it, and audit any new secrets or role changes since the 13th. And remember, tokens can stay valid for a while after you disable the app, so don't assume you're clear the moment you click the button.

And while we're on remote access tooling, CISA has added a BeyondTrust Remote Support flaw to its known exploited list. It's an authentication bypass, nine point eight critical, and it gives root on the appliance. Mandiant ties zero-day exploitation going back to late August to a suspected China-nexus espionage group, with web shells, persistence, credential harvesting and log deletion. SaaS tenants were patched automatically. If you run it yourself, apply the fix now, and if it was internet facing on a vulnerable version since August, treat it as possibly compromised. Patching doesn't remove persistence, so the advice is to rebuild from a clean image.

Elsewhere, F5 has pushed out-of-band patches for NGINX, including three critical bugs that can lead to code execution, though only on non-default configurations and nothing's been seen exploited yet. It covers NGINX Plus, open source, the Ingress Controller and friends. Worth getting into this week's change window.

A quick one for Exchange admins. Microsoft has confirmed that October's Windows Server updates can break on-prem Exchange under load, with Outlook on the web sign-in failures and mail stuck in outboxes. It's down to a new default limit on the Endpoint Mapper. There's a registry workaround while a proper fix is on the way, and Microsoft says don't uninstall the updates.

One more on patching. A chain of four Plex Media Server bugs, nicknamed Plex to Shell, is being exploited about a day after proof-of-concept code went public. Fixes shipped back in September. Attackers are dropping crypto miners and proxy software. Why should a business care about a home media server? Because it's often sitting on a staff member's home network, the same one their work laptop uses.

Now breaches. Nikkei has disclosed its third breach in about a month. It says stolen credentials were used to get into a development system on the 7th, exposing around one point eight million records, roughly a hundred and fifty thousand of them with names, emails, addresses or phone numbers. Nikkei says there's no evidence of misuse so far. The pattern's the same as most breaches this year, valid logins, not clever exploits. Closer to home, TFG London, owner of Hobbs, Whistles and Phase Eight, is now more than a week into its incident. The company has confirmed systems are down and it's told the authorities, but there's still no word on how attackers got in or whether data was taken, and no group has publicly claimed it.

Right, AI and security. OpenCode, the AI coding tool, says an intruder got into a legacy system on the night of the 16th, and that third-party AI provider keys saved in its hosted Console may have been exposed. It's emailed the affected users, about one and a half percent of Console users, and told them to revoke and replace their keys. Some users are reporting unexpected charges on their OpenAI, Anthropic and Google accounts, but those are their own claims, not confirmed findings. The lesson is that an AI API key is a bearer credential with a credit card attached. Deleting it from a tool doesn't revoke it. Give each tool its own key, set spend limits and alerts, and treat AI keys in your secrets scanning exactly like cloud keys.

Now, the business side. Crunchbase says cyber startups raised six point eight billion dollars in the third quarter, up about half on the previous quarter, but from fewer, bigger rounds, mostly AI and identity. The take for anyone building a services business? Investors are betting on identity and AI, so that's where your customers will be buying, and where they'll need help integrating it. Meanwhile here in the UK, the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill reaches Report Stage in the Lords next Monday, the 26th. Managed service providers are in scope, with incident reporting that starts with an initial notice within twenty-four hours. Today's ConnectWise story is exactly the kind of incident that regime is designed for, so if you're an MSP, start rehearsing how you'd report one.

And today's one to hunt. Look at your third-party apps in Microsoft 365, not just your users. The idea is simple. Backup, security and management apps sign in as service principals, usually from predictable vendor IP ranges and at predictable times. Pull service principal sign-ins and flag any app signing in from an IP it's never used before. Then separately, alert whenever an app is given a new client secret or added to a directory role, because that's exactly how an attacker turns a borrowed app into a permanent backdoor.

So, three things for today. One, if you use ConnectWise Cloud Backup, disable the app in every tenant and review its sign-ins and credential changes. Two, patch self-hosted BeyondTrust Remote Support, and rebuild it if it was exposed since August. Three, rotate any AI provider keys stored in OpenCode Console, and set spend alerts on all of them.

That's your lot. Have a good one.
