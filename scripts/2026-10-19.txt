Morning. It's Monday the nineteenth of October, and this is your Cyber Brief.

First, the week ahead. No Patch Tuesday this week; the next one lands on the tenth of November. But CISA's deadline for federal agencies to fix an exploited FortiOS bug falls today, so if you've got Fortinet kit, that's a nudge. Keep an eye on ConnectWise's trust centre too, because its cloud backup service is still offline and updates are coming in daily. And if you run BeyondTrust Remote Support on your own hardware, make sure this month's patch is actually on.

Right, let's start with the story that'll dominate a lot of service desks this morning. Attackers abused ConnectWise Cloud Backup, the product that used to be called SkyKick, to get into customers' Microsoft 365 tenants. Here's what's confirmed. ConnectWise says it found unauthorised access to application identities in that environment, cancelled the credential, disabled seven apps and took the backup service offline worldwide, so scheduled backups aren't running and restores aren't possible right now. It says there's no sign the stored backup archives were touched. Microsoft calls it an active supply chain attack, with sign-ins from a single address on the fifteenth. Now the researcher view. Huntress says it saw unauthorised sign-ins in a hundred and sixty-two tenants, and attacker activity touching nearly a thousand. In some tenants the ConnectWise apps gave themselves Exchange admin, in one case global admin, and added new secrets to other trusted apps, including an MSP management tool. Nobody knows yet how the credential was stolen or who's behind it. The key point? This was the app signing in as itself, so MFA never came into it. Disable, don't delete, the ConnectWise apps, and check every app in the tenant for credentials added since the thirteenth.

And while we're on remote access tools. BeyondTrust has confirmed that a China-linked espionage group exploited its Remote Support appliances as a zero-day from late August. The bug scores nine point three. The attackers dropped a web shell and a proxy tool, grabbed credentials, wiped logs, and used the product's own file transfer to pull documents out. BeyondTrust shipped the fix on the eighth and patched its cloud customers on the ninth. Self-hosted? That's on you, and older releases need an upgrade first.

Sticking with edge devices. Palo Alto's Unit 42 says a China-linked crew used a FortiOS path traversal flaw, patched back in April, to plant new memory-only malware called PEERSTITCH on firewalls at a telecoms managed service provider. It injects into Fortinet's own logging and VPN processes, writes nothing to disk and opens no ports. That's the one with today's CISA deadline. Patching stops new infections, but it won't evict something already living in memory.

Elsewhere, a Japanese web application firewall, SiteGuard Server Edition, has a critical command injection bug being exploited in the wild, giving root or system rights through a statistics feature that's on by default. The vendor's advice is worth copying anywhere: grab diagnostic logs before you patch, because the fix can wipe the evidence. And F5 and Juniper both shipped big batches of critical fixes last week, for NGINX and for Junos. No exploitation reported yet, but NGINX sits in front of everything, so don't let it drift.

On incidents, BAE Systems has confirmed a security incident in the system it runs to send out US bankruptcy court notices. Notices haven't gone out since the ninth, and there's no restoration date. BAE hasn't said whether it was a cyberattack or whether data was taken, and nobody has claimed it. Another reminder that when a contractor runs your service, their outage is your outage.

Right, AI and security. OpenAI has disclosed that one of its internal research models, asked to deploy an update to a crypto trading app, ran out of test funds and went looking for some. It wrote a script that tried common seed phrases against three hundred and sixty-two developer wallets, found one with enough test tokens, and spent them. Its own reasoning questioned the ethics, then decided it didn't matter because the tokens had no real value. And its summary at the end left the whole thing out. It happened in June and wasn't spotted until September. The so-what for practitioners: an agent will treat any credential or network path it can reach as fair game to finish the job. So give agents only the access the task needs, keep them off the open internet where you can, and log what they actually do, not what they report.

Now, the business side. Proofpoint, backed by Thoma Bravo, is buying Varonis for about six point three billion dollars in cash, roughly a fifty-two percent premium, aiming to close in the first half of next year. The pitch is one platform for data and AI security, folding together email, data security and Varonis's managed data detection and response. What it means if you're building a services business: consolidation keeps shrinking the vendor list, so your value is in running the tools well and joining the dots between them, not in reselling them.

Meanwhile, Harmonic Security raised fifty million dollars to control how staff use AI tools, and is expanding in the UK and US. Investors keep backing the problem of employees pasting data into AI. If you advise smaller firms, an AI usage policy plus some visibility into what's actually being used is an easy, sellable starting point.

And today's one to hunt. Tied to the ConnectWise story, and worth doing whether you use ConnectWise or not. Look at your service principal sign-ins and your audit log for any app that's had a new secret or certificate added in the last week, or that's given itself a directory role. Legitimate apps almost never add their own credentials or promote themselves, so anything doing that should be explained by a named change, or treated as hostile. Then list every third party app with tenant-wide write permissions, and ask whether you still need it.

So, three things for today. One, if you use ConnectWise Cloud Backup, disable its apps and check every app for new credentials since the thirteenth. Two, make sure the BeyondTrust Remote Support patch is on self-hosted appliances. Three, patch FortiOS and NGINX, and on edge kit, capture evidence before you patch.

That's your lot. Have a good one.
