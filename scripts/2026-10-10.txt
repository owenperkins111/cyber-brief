Morning. It's Saturday the 10th of October, and this is your Cyber Brief.

Right, let's start with patching, because there's a critical Atlassian bug that went from write-up to attack in about two hours.

It's a file-read flaw in the Data Center editions of eight Atlassian products, including Jira, Confluence, Bitbucket, Bamboo and Crowd. It's rated nine point three, and it needs no login. An attacker can pull specific files out of the web application's root. Why does that matter? Because researchers at watchTowr showed it can expose the Crowd credentials stored in Jira's config, and from there you can create users and promote a rogue account to Jira admin. Atlassian patched it on Monday, watchTowr published details, and honeypots saw exploitation attempts roughly two hours later. watchTowr now confirms in-the-wild exploitation, though so far it's mostly scanning for config and sensitive files rather than confirmed takeovers. Atlassian Cloud is already fixed. If you run Data Center, patch now. If you can't, take it off the internet or apply Atlassian's WAF or rewrite-rule mitigations, and if it was exposed, rotate the Crowd credentials anyway.

And while we're on edge devices, Citrix has patched another critical NetScaler bug. It's a memory overflow in NetScaler ADC and Gateway, scored nine point five, that can lead to remote code execution. The catch is it only bites when the appliance is set up as a SAML identity provider or service provider. There's no sign of exploitation yet, but three other NetScaler bugs are already being exploited, so this won't stay quiet for long. Check your config for SAML and get onto the fixed builds.

Elsewhere, the FBI and Secret Service have warned about the FortiBleed campaign against Fortinet firewalls and SSL VPNs. The notice cites research counting over eighty-six thousand compromised devices worldwide. Ransomware affiliates are using leaked and cracked credentials to log in, creating new admin accounts on the firewall, then moving into Active Directory. The new twist is lockout: attackers disabling accounts or changing passwords so victims can't get back into their own kit. The advice is to kill all admin and VPN sessions, reset credentials, enforce phishing-resistant MFA, and review firewall users and config for anything you didn't add.

Now breaches, and the big UK one this week is ASOS. On Tuesday, customers got a bizarre push notification through the ASOS app, addressed to the company's data protection officer, claiming its data had been compromised and demanding engagement. ASOS has now confirmed that an attacker got into an employee account by impersonating a trusted contact to get login details, then used those credentials to reach third-party platforms it uses to talk to customers. ASOS says names, contact details and some account information were taken, but not payment cards or passwords. The group, calling itself Xuanye, claims it got in through an AI marketing platform connected to ASOS's Snowflake environment. That's the attacker's claim, not ASOS's, and Snowflake says its own service wasn't at fault. The so-what? The weak point wasn't the data warehouse. It was a person, and then every connected SaaS tool that person's login could reach. And expect very convincing ASOS-themed phishing to follow.

On threat actors, the UK, the US and five allies have issued a joint advisory on Integrity Technology Group, a Beijing-based contractor linked to the group known as Flax Typhoon. The advisory says the company builds and sells hacking tools and runs infrastructure for Chinese state-backed operations. The NCSC says these actors combine AI-assisted scanning, big botnets and hands-on exploitation. Alongside it, the FBI seized seven domains tied to the group's scanning and phishing tools. Techniques to watch for include password spraying against Microsoft 365 and VPN software dropped in for persistence.

Right, AI and security.

CrowdStrike has tied a run of attacks on South Korean banks to a single, likely Chinese-speaking, financially motivated attacker who leaned heavily on AI. The main tool was an open-source agentic pentesting framework, backed by several large language models, and the attacker also used Claude Code. At least nine banks have disclosed attacks or been reported as targets, with one confirming around twenty-five thousand customers affected. The lesson is speed: one operator, many intrusions, a few weeks.

And Barracuda has seen phishing emails with hidden prompt injections aimed at the AI assistant that summarises your inbox, tucked into HTML comments, invisible text and zero-width characters. The goal is to make the assistant flag the email as urgent and legit. If you've rolled out an email copilot, strip hidden content before it reaches the model.

Now, the business side.

Rein Security has raised a twenty-five million dollar Series A for runtime security for AI agents, tracing each action an agent takes and blocking harmful ones before they run. That's another big cheque for agent security this autumn. The take for anyone building a services business: customers are deploying agents faster than they can govern them, and there's room for a service that inventories and watches them.

And closer to home, the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill hits Report Stage in the Lords on the 26th of October. Relevant managed service providers are firmly in scope, including new incident reporting duties. Nothing kicks in that day, and Royal Assent isn't expected before late this year at the earliest. But if you run an MSP, the time to tidy your own incident reporting and supplier security is before your customers start asking.

And today's one to hunt. Off the back of FortiBleed, look for new administrator accounts appearing on your firewalls and VPN gateways. The logic is simple: attackers who log in with stolen credentials almost always create a fresh admin account to keep their foothold. In a well-run estate, new firewall admins are rare and come with a change ticket. So pull the admin-creation events from your firewall logs over the past few months, and check every single one against a known change. Anything unexplained, treat as an incident.

So, three things for today. One, patch Atlassian Data Center now, because exploitation started within hours. Two, if your NetScaler uses SAML, get it onto the fixed build. Three, audit firewall and VPN admin accounts and sessions for anything you didn't create.

That's your lot. Have a good one.
