Morning. It's Thursday the fifteenth of October, and this is your Cyber Brief.

Right, let's start with the patching, because there's a lot of it. Microsoft's October Patch Tuesday landed this week with a record of over fourteen hundred fixes. The one to move on first is a Windows Shell bug that's being exploited right now. A crafted file sent by email, Teams or Slack can slip past Mark of the Web, so you don't get the usual warning or Protected View. It still needs someone to open the file, but that's never been much of a barrier, has it? CISA has given US agencies until tomorrow to fix it. After that, look at a critical SharePoint account hijack bug and the batch of wormable flaws in DHCP Server, Remote Desktop Gateway and Network Policy Server. And a heads up: this was the last update for Windows 11 24H2 Home and Pro.

The biggest edge story is Ivanti Connect Secure. Volexity found an attacker chaining three zero-days to get root on a VPN appliance that was fully patched at the time. Ivanti has fixed the authentication bypass and code execution part. The privilege escalation piece is still unpatched, and a fix is promised in the coming weeks. Once inside, the attacker pulled stored credentials, went Kerberoasting, and searched Active Directory for admin passwords and BitLocker recovery keys. They then cleaned the logs. Volexity says Ivanti's own integrity checker won't spot this chain, and honeypots are already logging exploit attempts from dozens of addresses. So patch, but if you've had an exposed box since early October, assume you need to investigate it properly.

And while we're on edge devices, Cisco's warning of active attacks on its Secure Email Gateway. It's rated nine point eight. One email with a crafted attachment can write files and run commands as root, with no login needed. You're exposed if File Reputation is switched on in a mail policy and the content scanner component is out of date. There's no workaround, so update. Palo Alto has also confirmed limited exploitation of a PAN-OS bug in the firewall management interface. It needs a login, but it gives root, and some fixes only arrive today. The so-what is the same as ever: management interfaces should never face the internet.

One more on the patching front. Attackers have been backdooring Nextcloud servers since September through the built-in Collabora document server app. Disabling the app doesn't protect you. You have to upgrade it or remove it, then go looking for stray PHP web shells in its folder.

Now, breaches. Here in the UK, TFG London, which owns Hobbs, Whistles and Phase Eight, has confirmed a cyber attack it spotted on Sunday. Stores are trading, but some websites and orders are disrupted. It's reported the incident to the ICO, and says there's no reason to think payment details are at risk. Nobody's claimed it yet. Elsewhere in retail, H&M says it's investigating ShinyHunters' claim that they stole inventory, logistics and sales data. H&M hasn't confirmed a breach and says it's seen no sign of compromise so far. With Black Friday coming, retail is clearly in the firing line.

Meta, meanwhile, has confirmed that an outside group used one employee's stolen credentials to get into some of its GitHub repositories. It says nearly all of what was taken was code, but personal data on around fifteen hundred people had been committed by mistake. Worth scanning your own repos for secrets and personal data.

On the law enforcement side, Microsoft and partners have run a court-authorised takedown of three phishing kits. Between them, those kits sent over a billion emails and texts this year, often from hijacked Microsoft 365 and Google tenants. And the US has shut down more than forty laptop farms and charged fourteen people over the North Korean IT worker scheme. The FBI says the workers are now faking stolen laptop reports to get replacement kit shipped to new addresses. Worth checking how your hiring and IT teams handle requests like that.

Right, AI and security. The researchers at XBOW have built a full Chrome exploit chain, from simply visiting a web page to running code on Windows outside both sandboxes. They did it in six days, using agents on a public OpenAI model, for roughly forty thousand dollars in compute. Google paid them a quarter of a million dollar bounty, and all three bugs are fixed. Just make sure Chrome has actually been restarted. In the same vein, Tencent used its own multi-agent system to find a Linux kernel flaw it calls Vghost. It gives any local user root, and in some virtualisation setups it lets a guest reach the host. The practical point is that the time between a bug existing and a working exploit is shrinking fast. So your patch windows for browsers and kernels need to shrink too.

Now, the business side. A Kaspersky survey out this week says eighty-seven percent of retailers had a cyber incident in the last year, and eighty-two percent are raising security budgets. A lot of that money is going to outside providers. If you sell into retail, lead with phishing resilience and recovery, not just tools. Separately, the MSPAlliance has updated its certification standard to version four. It now expects managed providers to vet and document any AI-enabled and third-party services before rolling them out. And the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill reaches Report Stage in the Lords on the twenty-sixth of October. Managed service providers stay firmly in scope. The take is simple: being able to show your own supply chain and AI controls is fast becoming part of the sale.

And today's one to hunt. Go back to the Ivanti chain. In the one case investigated, the attacker hammered the VPN's welcome login page, again and again. Those requests carried an unusual internal header that pointed at a different path from the one in the address. So look in your web or proxy logs for a burst of requests to that page, especially with that mismatched header. Then check your directory logs. Look for the VPN's service account suddenly running directory searches or asking for lots of Kerberos service tickets.

So, three things for today. One, patch the Windows Shell zero-day and check your Ivanti VPNs for compromise, not just their version. Two, if you run Cisco's email gateway, update it today, and get Palo Alto management interfaces off the internet. Three, AI is cutting the time from bug to exploit, so tighten your patch windows for browsers and Linux hosts.

That's your lot. Have a good one.
